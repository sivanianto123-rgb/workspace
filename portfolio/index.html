01 · Appeal Desk

Fast decisions on appeals, with the agent still in charge.

Support agents review thousands of creator appeals about strikes and monetization. A model can recommend an outcome in seconds. The design problem is making that recommendation useful without agents rubber-stamping it.

The problem

Appeals matter a lot to the people filing them. A wrong strike can stop a creator's income. Agents work under time targets, so any AI suggestion is tempting to accept without checking. That leads to automation bias: the model's mistakes become the agent's mistakes, at scale.

The goal was to make agents faster on clear cases and more careful on unclear ones, using the same interface.

Principles

Friction scales with risk

High confidence on a reversible action means one click. Low confidence on an income-affecting action means a short check first.

Show the reasons

Every recommendation lists the evidence for and against it, with a source for each item. Confidence alone is not a reason.

Disagreement is data

Overrides ask for a reason from a short list. That feeds model evaluation and shows where policy is unclear.

Key decisions

Explored

Auto-fill the decision. Fastest option, but it makes the AI's answer the default and turns review into proofreading.

Chose

Recommendation as a separate, labelled card. The agent's decision is always an explicit action, and approving is just one choice among four.

Explored

A bare percentage. "61%" means little under time pressure, and people read 61 and 92 as similar.

Chose

Banded confidence with color and a label (High / Review / Low), plus the number for people who want it.

Explored

A confirmation dialog on every action. People learn to click through dialogs within days.

Chose

A gate only on low-confidence, high-stakes cases, and a 6-second undo on everything else.

How I'd measure it

Override accuracy

When agents disagree with the AI, how often QA confirms the agent was right. This is the main sign that review is real.

Handle time by band

Should fall for High-confidence cases and may rise slightly for Low. Both are good outcomes.

Appeal reversal rate

How often creators win a second appeal after a first decision. Lower means fewer wrong first calls.

This is a concept project. Next step would be moderated sessions with 5–6 support agents on the prototype, focused on whether the evidence list changes their decisions.

02 · Channel Rescue

Getting a hacked channel back, without starting over at every step.

When a channel is hijacked, the creator is panicking and often locked out of their email too. Today that usually means forms, waiting and explaining the story again to each person. I redesigned the flow so AI does the triage and a specialist picks up with everything already gathered.

Show what has already been done, not just "we got your request". Progress lowers panic and repeat contacts.

Why a human stays in

Restoring access is the one step where a wrong call hands a channel to an attacker. Attackers also use recovery flows on purpose. So the AI gathers and scores evidence, and a specialist makes the access decision with the case file in front of them.

03 · Trust Patterns

Six patterns so every AI feature behaves the same way.

When several teams ship AI features separately, each invents its own way to show confidence or ask for review. Users then have to relearn trust on every screen. These patterns came out of the two projects above, written so other designers and engineers can reuse them.

Autonomy matches stakes

How much the AI may do comes from two questions: can it be undone, and how much does it affect the user? Teams pick from the grid, not from scratch.

How I work

From a fuzzy model capability to a shipped interaction.

1Frame

Agree with PM and engineering on the decision being made, by whom, and what a wrong call costs.

2Learn

Shadow the people doing the work. Read the support tickets. Find where trust breaks today.

3Map

Flows and swimlanes that show which steps belong to the AI and which to people.

4Prototype

Build with real model output early, using AI tools and code, so we test real behaviour, not mockups.

5Test

Watch whether people catch the model's mistakes, not only whether they finish the task.

6Ship & measure

Launch with feedback built in, and track override accuracy next to speed.
